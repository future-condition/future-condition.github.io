01 / THE QUESTION

The scene is right.
The timing is wrong.

Future-Experience Conditioning uses a generated 16-frame video to guide a robot through a task. But even when the object and goal are correct, the video may run ahead of or behind the physical execution. On eight CALVIN tasks [1], a five-frame early shift drops success from 69.8% to 34.2%. A policy without future guidance reaches 54.0%.

RAFC addresses the timing mismatch at the controller. It reuses a future generated once at task initialization, with no shift labels or online video regeneration.

02 / THE METHOD

One future.
Four ways to read it.

A static fallback and three local phases of the same clip pass through a shared frozen behavior-cloning policy. A learned gate selects useful dynamic information, and a bounded residual actor corrects the final action.

01

Generate once

Language grounding, robot-free digital-twin rollout, and video diffusion produce a task-consistent 16-frame future [2].

02

Compare phases

Evaluate a static first-frame fallback alongside dynamic candidates at offsets −2, 0, and +2.

03

Weight and act

The gate learns dynamic trust α and relative phase weights from task reward; the residual policy acts on the blended guidance.

What changes at each control step? +

The generated clip stays fixed. RAFC forms nearby temporal views of that clip, evaluates all four branches through the same frozen base policy, and uses the current observation to set the trust and phase mixture before residual correction.

04 / PHYSICAL ROBOT

From a controlled shift
to a real robot.

On a Franka Panda, RAFC improves all three manipulation tasks under natural timing variation. Twenty trials are conducted per task and method.

The physical trials do not impose or measure a timing offset; the controlled timing intervention is the CALVIN experiment above.
