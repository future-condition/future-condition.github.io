Reliability-Aware Future Conditioning for Temporally Robust Robot Manipulation

A generated future can show the right task at the wrong time. RAFC learns how much of that future to trust before each action.

The problem

The scene is right. The timing is wrong.

Future-conditioned policies use a short generated video to anticipate the task. If the robot moves faster or slower than that video, a plausible future can become harmful guidance. On eight CALVIN tasks, a five-frame early shift drops generated-future success from 69.8% to 34.2%, below the 54.0% policy without future guidance.

The approach

Test nearby phases, then decide what to trust

RAFC sits on top of Future-Experience Conditioning (FEC). One 16-frame future is generated at task initialization through language grounding, a robot-free digital-twin rollout, and video diffusion. The controller reuses that clip during execution; it does not regenerate video at every step.

01

Four candidate futures

A static first-frame fallback and three dynamic versions of the received clip at local offsets −2, 0, and +2. Every candidate runs through the same frozen behavior-cloning policy.

02

Reliability and phase

A learned gate sets dynamic trust α and weights the three temporal candidates. It blends policy features, future latents, and base actions without a shift label or alignment target.

03

Residual control

A bounded residual actor corrects the blended base action. The gate and actor learn jointly from task reward while the base behavior-cloning policy stays frozen.

Beyond boundary repetition

Distinct windows extracted from independently generated 28-frame sources remove the repeated-boundary-frame explanation. The shifted average rises from 69.7% to 75.6% with RAFC.

Under 0.75× and 1.25× rate warps, the average rises from 58.6% to 73.2%.

A timing change during execution

After an unannounced step-40 change from zero to ±3 or ±5 frames, average success is 65.7% with uniform averaging, 68.9% with a learned constant gate, and 75.4% with RAFC. The gate shifts candidate weight toward the compensating phase and lowers dynamic trust at the largest offsets.

Across benchmark scenes

On the five evaluated unshifted RoboCasa tasks, the FEC + RAFC system reaches 76.6% task-balanced success; CLOVER reaches 72.6%, SuSIE 63.5%, V2A 62.8%, and Diffusion Policy 39.3% in the paper's evaluation. These are system-level comparisons across different policy and future interfaces.
